02 · Connected Multiomics

Turning an acquisition into one connected product experience.

A product-integration case study spanning scientific workflows, requirements, verification, usability and Early Access readiness.

RoleTechnical Product Manager
StagePre-release through Early Access
ContextPost-acquisition product integration
DomainMultiomics scientific workflows

Problem statement

Two products needed to behave like one experience.

I joined Connected Multiomics before its first Early Access release, shortly after an acquisition. The challenge extended beyond connecting software. Requirements, scientific workflows, UX, testing and customer enablement all needed to converge into a product that felt coherent to researchers.

Solution

Connect the workflow, then close the launch-readiness gaps around it.

I translated scientific workflows into product requirements and user needs, helped reconcile requirements across the integrated products, partnered with test engineering on verification and traceability, contributed to UX and extensive UAT, and supported workflows spanning areas such as spatial, single-cell RNA and proteomics.

Usability work exposed where researchers naturally expected actions and where a technically functional flow still created friction. Those findings fed back into product behavior before Early Access.

My role

I worked across product definition, quality and customer readiness.

My role sat between product, engineering, test engineering and the scientific user. I wrote requirements and user needs, worked through test coverage and traceability, participated deeply in UAT and customer demos, and identified customer-facing gaps that did not have an obvious owner.

Scientific workflowsProduct requirementsDesign controlsUATTest traceabilityUsabilityCustomer demosLaunch readiness

Key challenge

The software could be releasable and still not be usable.

As Early Access approached, documentation and customer enablement did not have a clear owner. I created the documentation experience and supporting demos rather than treating them as work outside the product boundary. The goal was to make the integrated workflow understandable once it reached a real customer, not simply to satisfy a release checklist.

Product lessonThe last mile is part of the product. Requirements, verification, usability, documentation and customer learning all contribute to whether a technical product actually works in practice.

Skills & takeaways

What I carried forward.

Scientific empathy

My biology background helped me reason from the research workflow instead of treating domain complexity as an abstraction.

Quality partnership

Requirements become stronger when product and test engineering think about expected behavior and traceability together.

Proactive ownership

When an important customer gap had no obvious owner, I treated that as a product problem to solve.

Usability

Watching users work can reveal product gaps that are invisible in a requirements document.
