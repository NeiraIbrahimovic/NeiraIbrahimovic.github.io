01 · The problem

Infrastructure behavior becomes product behavior at enterprise scale.

The platform had to make cloud primitives usable and governable for enterprise customers. Identity, storage movement, deletion and shared compute all created customer-facing behavior even when the underlying services were invisible.

PRODUCT QUESTIONHow do we turn infrastructure constraints into coherent product contracts customers can adopt?

02 · My role & impact

I helped shape IAM role migration, egress metering and commercialization, recoverable deletion, and contributed to fair scheduling. 04 · Key product decisions

The tradeoffs that shaped the work.

DECISION 01Migration is part of architecture.+

A technically cleaner target state can still fail as a product if existing customers cannot get there.

THE DECISION

Design backward compatibility and adoption paths alongside the future-state architecture.

DECISION 02Commercialization starts in telemetry.+

Pricing depends on the system observing and attributing the behavior being monetized.

THE DECISION

Treat metering and identity semantics as prerequisites to a customer-facing usage model.

DECISION 03Infrastructure policies are customer experience.+

Lifecycle and scheduler rules may be invisible, but customers feel their effects through reliability, wait time and predictability.

THE DECISION

Define infrastructure behavior with the same product rigor as application features.
