04 · CLOUD ECONOMICS · USAGE-BASED EGRESS

Turning infrastructure cost into product behavior.

How I helped define the metering, attribution and commercialization model needed to make cloud data egress measurable and billable.

RoleTechnical Product Manager
StageProduct strategy + requirements
EnvironmentAWS enterprise platform
FocusMetering · attribution · commercialization

01 · The problem

Infrastructure cost did not yet have customer-facing semantics.

Data could move across platform boundaries in several ways. Before egress could become a customer-facing model, we needed to define what movement counted, who received the usage, and what telemetry downstream billing could rely on.

PRODUCT QUESTIONHow do we turn cloud-provider economics into usage customers can understand and the platform can reliably measure?

02 · My role & impact

I helped shape the product and commercialization model.

I worked through usage definitions, attribution, event-driven usage records and commercialization considerations with engineering and commercial stakeholders. I moved to SIMS before the full implementation was delivered, so my contribution was product definition and strategy rather than end-to-end launch.

04 · Key product decisions

The tradeoffs that shaped the work.

DECISION 01Define the billable event before the price.+

A pricing model is unreliable if the system has not defined exactly which behavior creates usage.

THE DECISION

Separate customer-attributable egress from internal platform movement before discussing price.

DECISION 02Separate measurement from aggregation.+

Raw platform activity and a customer's bill are different layers of the system.

THE DECISION

Create reliable usage records first so downstream systems can aggregate them consistently.

DECISION 03Make attribution explicit.+

Shared data and actions performed on behalf of users complicated who should receive usage.

THE DECISION

Define attribution semantics in the product requirements rather than leaving them implicit.
