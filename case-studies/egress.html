04 · Cloud economics

Turning cloud egress into a customer-facing product model.

A platform commercialization case study in metering, attribution, event-driven usage and pricing semantics.

RoleTechnical Product Manager
ProblemAWS data movement created cost without a customer-facing usage model
GoalDefine measurable and billable product behavior
FocusMetering, attribution and commercialization

The problem

What made this difficult.

Data could move across platform boundaries in several ways, but the platform needed product semantics before infrastructure cost could become a customer-facing model. We had to define what movement counted, who should receive the usage, and what events the system needed to emit so downstream billing could work.

Key product decisions

The decisions underneath the feature.

01

Define the billable event before the price

The first question was not the dollar amount. It was which data movements represented customer-attributable egress and which were internal platform behavior.

02

Separate measurement from aggregation

Platform activity needed to produce reliable usage records that downstream systems could aggregate and eventually bill.

03

Design attribution for shared workflows

Data sharing and actions performed on behalf of users complicated ownership. The product requirements had to make attribution explicit.

04

Translate provider economics into product behavior

Cloud-provider tiering had to become a model customers could understand rather than exposing raw infrastructure complexity.

My role & impact

Where I contributed.

I helped shape the product strategy and requirements for egress metering and billing, working through usage definitions, attribution, event-driven records and commercialization considerations with engineering and commercial stakeholders. I transitioned to SIMS before the full implementation was delivered, so my ownership here was in product definition and strategy rather than end-to-end launch.

Technical deep dive

What I had to understand to make the product decisions.

This work connected product pricing to architecture. If the platform cannot observe the right event, identify the responsible customer and preserve enough context for aggregation, a pricing model cannot be implemented reliably.

What I learned

Commercialization can start deep in the system architecture. Pricing decisions often create telemetry, identity and attribution requirements long before a customer ever sees a bill.
