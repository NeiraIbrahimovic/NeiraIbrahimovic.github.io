01 · SIMS · Enterprise data platform

Building a global identity layer for samples.

A 0→1 platform case study in data modeling, APIs, legacy-system compatibility and cross-functional execution across a genomics software ecosystem.

RoleProduct Lead / Technical Product Manager
OwnershipVision through internal beta
Integration surface12+ product teams
UsersLab scientists, bioinformaticians and downstream applications

The problem

One biological sample. Multiple software identities.

Illumina's software ecosystem had grown across products that created and represented samples differently. A sample planned for a run could become disconnected from the files, analyses and downstream interpretation associated with it. Customers and internal teams often compensated with manual reconciliation and spreadsheets.

The product opportunity was not another sample screen. It was a shared identity and data layer that could preserve lineage as a sample moved through the workflow.

My scope

I owned the product problem end to end.

I took SIMS from an early concept into a defined platform product. My scope included product vision and roadmap, the enterprise sample model, API and integration requirements, UX and prototypes, customer discovery, alpha and beta readiness, launch planning and coordination across more than a dozen integrating teams.

That meant operating at several levels at once: aligning senior leaders on why the platform mattered, going deep enough with architects and engineers to make data-model decisions, and translating those decisions into behavior that downstream product teams could adopt.

Challenge 01 · Data model

The cleanest new model would have broken the old world.

A greenfield design could define a single ideal sample object. We did not have a greenfield system. Existing products already had years of customer workflows built around their own semantics.

One of the most important decisions involved compatibility with BaseSpace. Its existing model distinguished run-level Samples from Biosamples that could aggregate across runs. Rather than force an ecosystem-wide rewrite, we established a mapping in which a SIMS Run Sample represented the run-specific instance and a SIMS Sample represented the persistent identity that could span runs.

Product principleOptimize the target architecture and the migration path together. A technically elegant platform that nobody can adopt is not a successful platform.

Challenge 02 · Integration execution

A platform ships on everybody else's calendar too.

SIMS depended on products with different release cadences, security requirements and integration patterns. During an early release cycle, one integrating product had a structured code freeze that did not align with SIMS development. Web-component and security-readiness work landed too close to that boundary.

I chose to prioritize an internal beta before external exposure rather than treat the original date as the only measure of success. The lesson changed how I approached later platform planning: code freezes, security scans, version dependencies and downstream release mechanics need to be modeled as product dependencies from the start.

Challenge 03 · Migration

The hardest work lived between team boundaries.

As the new model became real, the ecosystem needed a migration strategy for existing sample data and API behavior. The work crossed several systems, which meant there was no obvious single owner.

When international teams met in San Diego, I created the working agenda and brought a proposed migration approach into the room rather than beginning from a blank page. Engineers challenged and refined it, and the group left with a concrete direction. That became an important operating pattern for me: when ownership is distributed, make the problem tangible enough for the right people to make a decision.

What this changed in how I work

Platform PM became less about features and more about interfaces.

Senior leadership communication

Translate deep architectural work into the customer problem, strategic value, decision needed and risk without losing the substance.

Cross-functional execution

Roadmaps for platform products must include other teams' release mechanics, security work and migration dependencies, not just your own backlog.

Technical product judgment

Data models and API semantics are product decisions when they determine what customers and downstream systems can do.

Influence without authority

Integration teams had their own priorities. Adoption required a clear contract, a credible migration path and repeated alignment rather than simply assigning work.

“One of the hardest and longest-standing problems at Illumina.”

A colleague's description of the sample problem. It stayed with me because the difficulty was not a single feature. It was creating shared behavior across an ecosystem that had evolved independently for years.
