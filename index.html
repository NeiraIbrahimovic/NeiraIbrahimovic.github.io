01

0→1 enterprise data platform

So... what exactly is a sample?

This turned out to be a surprisingly difficult question. Different products had different answers. The same biological sample could become disconnected as it moved through a workflow. Customers learned to stitch the story back together themselves.

Usually with spreadsheets. Lots of spreadsheets.

I owned SIMS from an early concept into a real product. That meant figuring out what the product should be, shaping the data model, working through API behavior, testing ideas with customers and coordinating integrations across more than a dozen teams.

Run planning→Sample→Analysis→Interpretation

The part that looked simple but wasn’t

We wanted one sample identity that could move across the workflow without breaking the systems customers already depended on. That meant the ideal model had to meet years of legacy behavior somewhere in the middle.

Someone from another team once told me I was working on one of the hardest and longest standing problems at Illumina. I still think about that.

02

Multiomics

Making two products feel like one.

I joined Connected Multiomics before its first Early Access release, shortly after an acquisition. My job quickly became much bigger than integrating requirements.

I wrote product requirements and user needs, worked closely with test engineering on traceability, contributed to UX and UAT and helped bring different scientific workflows into one cohesive experience.

I also noticed we did not have the customer documentation we needed for launch. There was no clear owner, so I built it.

03

Cloud platform

Deleting a file sounds easy.

It was not.

I worked across the infrastructure behind an AWS based data platform. The problems ranged from secure customer storage access to data movement costs and recoverable deletion.

I helped shape requirements for moving customer owned storage toward IAM role based access. I also worked through egress metering and pricing, data lifecycle behavior and file integrity.

Some of this work continued after I moved to SIMS, so I am careful about the distinction between what I helped define and what I personally delivered.
