How I work

Give me a messy problem, a good team, and a whiteboard.

I work best collaboratively. When a problem gets complicated, I like making it visible, whether that means pulling up a prototype, mapping the architecture, sketching the workflow, or writing down the decisions we need to make. Give the team something concrete to react to, and we can usually get to a better answer faster.

01Intellectual curiosityAsk why. Then go one layer deeper.+

I genuinely love learning how things work. That curiosity has taken me from molecular biology to computer science to product management, and it pushes me to keep asking questions until I understand the problem well enough to make a thoughtful product decision.

curiosity → clarity
02Attention to detailTurn complexity into clear requirements.+

I work on products with complex data models and integrations, where small details can have significant downstream effects. I like digging into expected behavior, edge cases, and acceptance criteria so engineering has the clarity and context needed to build the right solution.

the edge cases matter
03Cross-functional executionKeep the moving pieces moving together.+

The products I work on often depend on many teams and systems. I keep requirements, decisions, dependencies, risks, owners, and timelines organized so everyone stays aligned and complex initiatives keep moving forward across engineering, quality, product, and other partners.

organization = key
04Proactive problem solvingSee a blocker? Start solving.+

If I see something blocking the team, my instinct is to jump in rather than wait for someone else to own it. I start asking questions, bring the right people together, and work through possible solutions, even when the issue does not fit neatly inside my area of ownership.

jump in + move it forward

AI in practice

AI is part of how I work.

I use AI throughout my product workflows to move from an idea to something tangible faster. I build prototypes with live API integrations so they pull from real data instead of relying only on static mockups. That gives customers something realistic to react to early and lets me iterate quickly before engineering invests in the full solution.

I also look for repetitive operational work that AI can remove. I built an AI-assisted workflow to automate parts of the release-management cycle, turning product inputs into structured release artifacts for human review and saving 10+ hours of manual work each week.

For me, AI is most useful when it shortens the distance between an idea, real customer feedback and a better product decision.
