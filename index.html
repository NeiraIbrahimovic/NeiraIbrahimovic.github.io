AI in practice

I went to an AI workshop. Then I built something.

I attended an AI workshop with Microsoft looking for practical ways AI could improve the work we were already doing. I came back to a release-management process with a lot of repetitive documentation and coordination and started experimenting.

I prototyped an AI-assisted workflow that could take product inputs and turn them into structured release artifacts for human review. What started as an experiment became a workflow that saves 10+ hours of manual work each week.

The useful part was not “adding AI.” It was starting with a workflow people already found painful and figuring out where AI actually belonged.

The career plan was not exactly linear.

UCLA

Scientist first.

I studied molecular biology and mathematical biology. I spent time in the wet lab and learned what it feels like to be the person actually using scientific tools and working with the data they create.

Illumina internship

Then I got curious about the tools.

I worked on sequencing instrument validation and verification. Eventually I realized I was just as interested in building the products around the science as I was in the science itself.
